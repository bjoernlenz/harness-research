# Permissioned Agency

Permissioned agency begins with an uncomfortable truth: the moment an agent can really act, it can also really harm. This concept marks the family of harnesses that try to preserve usefulness without pretending that power is harmless.

Claude Code, Cursor, and OpenHands each stage that tension differently, but they share the same human problem. Developers want help that is strong enough to matter and contained enough to trust. Permissions are not only security controls here. They are emotional design. They tell the human, “you do not have to surrender the room in order to gain leverage.”

That is why permissioned agency often feels calmer than fully fluid autonomy, even when the underlying capabilities are large. The harness is making a social promise, not just a technical one. It is acknowledging that action should cross visible thresholds.

What this reveals is that trust in modern coding agents is often built through boundary choreography rather than raw intelligence. What it obscures is the friction cost. Too many approval moments produce fatigue, and too few turn permission into theater. The hard part is not merely adding boundaries; it is placing them where humans actually feel the difference.
